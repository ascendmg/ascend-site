Beyond the funnel

Product discovery

Sessions → add-to-cart

What the data shows

600 of 10,000 sessions (6%) included an add-to-cart action. This is where browsing turns into buying intent.
600 ÷ 10,000 = 6% add-to-cart rate

What we would investigate

  • Which collections and product pages sessions reach, and where they leave
  • Whether search and filters return useful results, including searches with no results
  • How navigation and product pages work on phones
  • Whether sizing, shipping and product details answer common questions

How HQAscend could help

  • Collection structure and navigation built around how customers shop
  • Better search, filters and mobile navigation, within the platform’s capabilities
  • Clearer product information, images and options
  • Product-level reporting so these questions can be answered with data

Cart to checkout

Add-to-cart → checkout

What the data shows

300 of the 600 sessions with an add-to-cart action (50%) reached checkout. Some carts are used to save items or compare prices, so a gap here is normal; the question is how large it is for this store.
300 ÷ 600 = 50% reached checkout

What we would investigate

  • Whether delivery costs and times are visible before checkout
  • Whether the cart is easy to review and edit on mobile
  • Whether the path from cart to checkout is clear
  • How this rate differs by device and traffic source

How HQAscend could help

  • Show shipping and returns information earlier
  • A simpler cart and clearer checkout button
  • Event tracking for cart and checkout steps

Checkout completion

Checkout → purchase

What the data shows

180 of the 300 sessions that reached checkout purchased, a 60% completion rate. 120 sessions did not complete. The data shows where sessions stopped, not why they stopped.
180 ÷ 300 = 60% checkout completion

What we would investigate

  • Unexpected shipping costs
  • Unclear delivery information
  • Checkout errors, especially on mobile
  • Payment friction, such as missing payment options

How HQAscend could help

  • Clearer shipping, delivery and returns information
  • Checkout configuration within the platform’s capabilities
  • Reliable tracking of each checkout step so errors can be found and measured

Order value

Revenue per order and per session

What the data shows

The 180 orders produced $14,400 in revenue, an average order value of $80 and $1.44 of revenue per session.
$14,400 ÷ 180 = $80 per order · $14,400 ÷ 10,000 = $1.44 per session

What we would investigate

  • Which products are often bought together
  • Whether product pages suggest relevant add-ons
  • Whether bundles or shipping thresholds fit the catalog
  • Which products actually earn profit, not just revenue

How HQAscend could help

  • Relevant product recommendations
  • Bundles
  • Clearer merchandising on collection and product pages

A higher order value does not automatically mean higher profit. Discounts, shipping, returns and product costs all affect margin. Revenue is not profit.

Customer retention

Repeat purchases from past customers

What the data shows

Separate cohort: 120 customers who made a first purchase in the 90 days before this example month. Of them, 18 placed a second order within 90 days of their first, a 15% repeat purchase rate. These customers are measured over their own 90-day windows, not the month above.
18 ÷ 120 = 15% repeat purchase rate

What we would investigate

  • Whether new customers receive useful order, delivery and review emails
  • Which first purchases lead to repeat orders
  • Whether campaigns match what customers actually bought
  • Whether reordering is easy

How HQAscend could help

  • Post-purchase email flows such as confirmation, delivery updates and review requests
  • Relevant email campaigns based on past purchases
  • Reporting that separates new and returning customers
